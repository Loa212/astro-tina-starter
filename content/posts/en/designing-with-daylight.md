Every project in the studio starts with the same drawing: a plan of the site with the path of the
sun traced across it in summer and in winter. Before we think about rooms, we think about **where
the light will be** at breakfast, in the late afternoon, on a grey day in January.

## Light first, walls second

A kitchen that faces east gets the low morning sun when you need it most. A living room that opens
to the west catches the last warm light of the day. Bedrooms are calmer on the north side, where
the light is even and soft. None of this is new, but it is surprisingly often forgotten once the
floor plan starts filling up.

> Daylight is the one material we never have to buy, and it changes the house every hour.

## What it means in practice

- **Deep reveals and overhangs** that let the winter sun in and keep the summer sun out
- **Windows placed for views and light**, not to fill a facade pattern
- **Light, matte surfaces** that bounce daylight further into the plan
- **Rooms that borrow light** from each other through internal glazing and high openings

The result is a home that needs less artificial light, stays cooler in summer and simply feels
better to be in. It is the cheapest decision we make, and often the one clients notice first.
